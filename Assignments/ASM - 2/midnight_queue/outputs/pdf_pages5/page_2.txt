C. The Full Ranking Tournament
Five full-sort contenders × six conditions × four sizes (120 cells; 14 genuine DNF cells). Every timed cell is five repetitions in its own worker, and four cells ran concurrently,
one per physical core; the fresh input copy is outside the timer. Absolute times therefore include four-way cache/memory contention, so compare cells within this table rather
than with the single-process D/E tables. The figure uses C2 for growth and n=10,000 for the condition comparison. The legend in panel (a) applies to all six panels.
Fig. C1. (a) Median time vs n on C2, log–log (s vs transactions); (b) median time at n=10,000 by condition, log scale (s); (c) comparisons and (d) moves vs n on C2, log scale (counts); (e) time/(n log2 n) and (f) time/n² on C2.
Missing points are DNF.
What happened at n=10,000 (fastest / slowest): C1: Hardened Quick Sort / Insertion Sort; C2: Hardened Quick Sort / Insertion Sort; C3: Insertion Sort / Naive Quick Sort;
C4: Heap Sort / Insertion Sort; C5: Merge Sort / Insertion Sort; C6: Hardened Quick Sort / Insertion Sort. Insertion Sort is linear on pre-ranked C3 but quadratic on C4; the
reverse and duplicate-heavy cases separate n log n from quadratic behaviour. On C5, 1% displacement already creates 34,333,390 inversions at n=100,000.
A clash worth stating. At n=10,000 insertion sort on C4 performs 49,995,000 comparisons versus 24,987,904 on C1, exactly the inversion prediction, yet its C1 median is
46.08s against 35.70s on C4. The five C1 runs drift from 36.84s to 56.18s under sustained load; this is thermal/scheduling noise, not an algorithmic reversal. Counts are the
stable evidence; times are reported exactly as measured.
