D. The Shift Queue (Top-K)
Three ways to produce the ordered K-queue: (a) hardened full sort then prefix, (b) the size-K heap (the root is the worst retained record), and (c) the bounded sorted buffer
with binary-search insertion and explicit shifting; its first K records are sorted once with our merge sort. Full-sort cost is independent of K and is measured once per input;
prefix extraction is negligible. The fraud curve uses Class only after the queue is built. The figure covers both required sizes and both C1 and C2.

Fraud capture. The K=52 queue contains 0 of 492 frauds (0.0%). At K=500 it captures 63 (12.8%); at K=2,068 it captures 246 (50.0%); at K=5,000, 361 (73.4%); at
K=10,000, 405 (82.3%). Producing the half-fraud queue costs the times in Table D1. Across the 28 measured cells the buffer is fastest in only three: both 100,000-record
K=52 cells and full-size C1/K=10 by 0.14 ms. The heap is fastest in the other 25, and the full sort never wins for K≤n/10. The ethical/operational clash is explicit: a 52-case
shift queue is cheap, but under this fixed scoring rule it surfaces no fraud; meeting the 50%-capture objective needs K≈2,068.
E. The Live Feed
The file is split into consecutive Time windows from the first transaction. After every window the committed queue is the top K of everything seen so far. Strategy A re-ranks
the whole prefix; B offers only new records to a maintained size-K heap; C Merge-Sorts the new window and merges it into the current K-list. Every published queue is
checked against an independent reference. Reported time is the complete 48-hour simulation; memory is a separate tracemalloc run. The 10-minute re-rank exceeded the
300 s watchdog on its first repetition, so it is a genuine DNF with no median or memory row; the other two strategies completed five runs.
